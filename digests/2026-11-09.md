# Regulations.gov comments, Nov 9–15, 2026

106,518 comments were posted across 275 dockets, 49% more than the week before. Most of them went to a few places: the top 3 dockets drew 72% of the week's total.
"Posted" means the day the agency made a comment public, not the day it was submitted, and agencies often post in batches, so a big week can be a backlog being released.

### Request for Information: Categories Used in Federal Vaccine Recommendations and the Role of Shared Clinical...

Department of Health and Human Services. 35,554 comments were posted this week, none the week before, and 35,570 so far. The comment period ended Sep 21. 100% of the week's posts came on Nov 10, so this looks like a batch release.

In a random sample of 25, 10 were unclear, 8 supported and 7 opposed; a script found no near-copies among them.

Commenters split between those defending the existing childhood vaccine schedule and those wanting more parental choice and a reduced schedule. Supporters of the current system stress that vaccines save lives, that outbreaks like measles are returning, and that extra categories or shared decision-making defaults would add confusion, burden clinics and risk insurance coverage. Others argue for informed consent, bodily autonomy and parental rights, oppose any mandates, and some want more vaccines, such as HPV and varicella, moved to shared decision-making. Many entries are a identical form-letter urging careful consideration without stating a position, and one is just an insult.

### Evaluation of the California Coastal Management Program; Notice of Public Meetings; Request for Comments

National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. 31,705 comments were posted this week, none the week before, and 31,705 so far. 100% of the week's posts came on Nov 12, so this looks like a batch release. The first comment was posted Nov 12.

In a random sample of 25, 23 supported, 1 opposed and 1 were unclear; 16 of them were near-copies of other comments in the sample, which points to form letters.

Nearly all commenters defend California's Coastal Management Program and oppose any move to weaken it, treating the federal evaluation as unnecessary or a pretext for pressure. Many argue the review is redundant because NOAA looked at the program recently, and that it threatens the state's federal consistency authority and federal funding. Others stress the coast's ecological value and its multibillion-dollar economy, and warn of risks from offshore oil, pipelines, and other development. One commenter takes the opposite view, criticizing California's regulation and calling for oil drilling to bring jobs and lower fuel prices. One submission is only an attachment cover note with no stated position.

### National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska Production Site Development

Bureau of Land Management. 9,149 comments were posted this week, up from 7,237, and 81,364 so far. The comment period ended Nov 10. 90% of the week's posts came on Nov 9, so this looks like a batch release.

In a random sample of 25, all 25 opposed; 23 of them were near-copies of other comments in the sample, which points to form letters.

All of the sampled commenters oppose the proposed permitting shortcut for oil and gas production sites in the National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska. Many comments are form-letter text. They argue that each project needs its own full environmental review under NEPA, including cumulative impacts, and that BLM should keep its authority to deny, condition or revoke approvals. They also stress protecting wildlife habitat such as birds, caribou and wetlands, along with subsistence resources and the climate. Several call for keeping public input and Tribal consultation, and criticize the rule as an industry-driven rubber stamp.
