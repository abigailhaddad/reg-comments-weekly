## What commenters are saying

A small random sample from each docket, not a poll. A model labeled each comment and wrote the summary; the counts and the form-letter check are computed by script, and a second model labeled stance again as a check.

### USBC-2026-0628: Decennial Census of the Population of Americans; Proposed Residence Criteria and Propos...
A random sample of 25 of the 37,741 comments posted this week.
Stance (model labels): oppose 24, support 1. The second model agreed on 25 of 25.
Near-identical text (by script): 16 of 25 sampled comments fall in 6 groups of near-copies, which suggests form letters.

Nearly all of these commenters oppose the proposed census rule and ask the Bureau to withdraw it. They argue that the Constitution and the 14th Amendment require counting all residents regardless of immigration status, and that excluding people would distort apportionment and federal funding. Others object to the winter-residence approach for snowbirds, saying it would shift seats and money away from colder states, and to dropping race and ethnicity questions, which they say are needed for civil rights enforcement, health planning, and community visibility. A few ask for a longer comment period. One commenter supports the rule, saying noncitizens should not count toward apportionment and that a citizenship question should be added.

- Constitution requires counting all residents regardless of immigration status (11 of 25)
- Removing race and ethnicity questions harms civil rights, health and equity data (9 of 25)
- Inaccurate count would reduce federal funding and local planning ability (9 of 25)
- Winter-residence rule would shift seats and funds away from colder states (6 of 25)
- Request for extended comment period (2 of 25)

### BLM-2026-0133: National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska Production Site Development
A random sample of 25 of the 15,696 comments posted this week.
Stance (model labels): oppose 25. The second model agreed on 25 of 25.
Near-identical text (by script): 24 of 25 sampled comments fall in 2 groups of near-copies, which suggests form letters.

Every comment in this sample opposes the proposed streamlined permitting for oil and gas production sites in the National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska. Most use a shared template that calls the plan a shortcut and asks for full, project-specific environmental review under NEPA, including cumulative impacts, along with continued public input and Tribal consultation. A second template focuses on bird habitat, especially the Teshekpuk Lake Special Area, and on caribou, whales, polar bears and Indigenous communities. Both groups raise concerns about wildlife, wetlands, subsistence resources and climate change in a rapidly warming Arctic.

- Harm to wildlife, wetlands, water and subsistence resources (25 of 25)
- Climate change and rapid Arctic warming (24 of 25)
- Need for full project-specific NEPA review and cumulative impact analysis (18 of 25)
- Preserve public input and Tribal consultation (18 of 25)
- Criticism of administration's Arctic drilling push (17 of 25)
- Protect bird habitat and the Teshekpuk Lake Special Area (7 of 25)

### EBSA-2026-0331: Electronic Disclosure by Group Health Plans Under ERISA
A random sample of 25 of the 8,733 comments posted this week.
Stance (model labels): oppose 25. The second model agreed on 25 of 25.
Near-identical text (by script): none found in the sample.

Every comment in this sample opposes the proposed rule and asks the Department of Labor to withdraw or reconsider it. The central objection is that the rule would turn silence into consent by making electronic delivery the default, so people would have to opt out to keep paper copies of HIPAA, COBRA and benefit documents. Commenters say seniors, rural residents, low-income households, people with disabilities, military members and the cybersecurity-wary would be hit hardest. They also argue that the rule is driven by insurer savings rather than consumer benefit, that no evidence shows people read and understand electronic notices as well as paper, and that people who want electronic delivery can already opt in.

- Vulnerable groups (seniors, rural, low-income, disabled, military) would be disproportionately harmed (15 of 25)
- Silence should not count as consent; keep affirmative opt-in (14 of 25)
- Rule is driven by insurer cost savings, not consumer benefit (11 of 25)
- No evidence electronic delivery gives equal comprehension and engagement (10 of 25)
- Burden shifts to consumers to opt out and maintain paper (10 of 25)
- Current system already lets electronic-preferring people opt in (10 of 25)
