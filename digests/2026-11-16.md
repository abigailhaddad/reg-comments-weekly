# Regulations.gov comments, Nov 16–22, 2026

35,704 comments were posted across 294 dockets, 67% fewer than the week before. Most of them went to a few places: the top 3 dockets drew 74% of the week's total.
"Posted" means the day the agency made a comment public, not the day it was submitted, and agencies often post in batches, so a big week can be a backlog being released.

### Electronic Disclosure by Group Health Plans Under ERISA

Employee Benefits Security Administration. 16,504 comments were posted this week, up from 1,832, and 79,187 so far. The comment period ended Sep 22. 61% of the week's posts came on Nov 17, so this looks like a batch release.

In a random sample of 25, all 25 opposed; a script found no near-copies among them.

Every commenter in this sample opposes the proposed shift to electronic delivery of group health plan notices as the default. They argue that treating inaction as consent is not real consent and that people should affirmatively choose electronic delivery. Many say seniors, rural residents, lower-income households, people with disabilities, military members and security-wary people would be hurt most, because they would need to navigate opt-out steps across several insurers. Others see the rule as driven by insurer cost savings rather than consumer benefit, and say there is no evidence that people switched automatically will read and understand the notices. Most ask that the current opt-in framework stay, with paper as the default, and a few short comments simply say they depend on paper mail.

### Cutting Administrative Requirements for Excellence (CARE) in Patient Care. CMS-3484-P

Centers for Medicare&Medicaid Services. 5,986 comments were posted this week, up from 1,009, and 6,995 so far. Comments are open until Jan 12. The first comment was posted Nov 12.

In a random sample of 25, all 25 supported; 20 of them were near-copies of other comments in the sample, which points to form letters.

All of these commenters respond to the anesthesia supervision request for information and urge CMS to drop the federal physician supervision requirement for nurse anesthetists. They argue the requirement lacks a statutory basis, that many states have already opted out, and that research shows no safety or outcome difference without supervision. They say the rule restricts access to anesthesia, particularly in rural and underserved areas, and raises costs by forcing duplicate staffing. A few add personal accounts of supervision being purely a billing formality, or argue that collaboration should follow clinical judgment rather than a federal mandate.

### Removing 30-Day Refugee Termination Notice

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. 4,099 comments were posted this week, down from 7,983, and 12,967 so far. The comment period ended Nov 14. 96% of the week's posts came on Nov 16, so this looks like a batch release.

In a random sample of 25, all 25 opposed; 25 of them were near-copies of other comments in the sample, which points to form letters.

All of the sampled comments are the same form letter, and all of them ask DHS to withdraw the interim final rule and restore the 30-day response window for refugees facing a notice of intent to terminate. They argue that ten days, counted from the mailing date rather than the receipt date, is too little time to understand a legal notice, find a lawyer, and collect evidence from countries the refugees fled. They stress the severe consequences: loss of status and work authorization, possible removal proceedings, effects on family members, and no appeal. They also say DHS has not justified the change, since the process has been stable for decades and the agency can already pursue removal directly. One version adds a religious appeal to treat refugees as fellow human beings.
